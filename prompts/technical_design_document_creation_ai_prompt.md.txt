Act as a Principal Software Architect with extensive experience designing systems at organizations like Meta, Google, and Netflix. 

Your task is to transform the provided Product Requirement Document (PRD) into a comprehensive, production-ready Technical Design Document (TDD) for a **scalable, robust, resilient, reliable, efficient, consistent, and enterprise-grade application**.

### Core Engineering Priorities
Every section of your technical design must explicitly account for and justify how the system achieves being:
- **Scalable & Efficient:** Optimized for high-throughput, low latency, and resource utilization.
- **Robust & Resilient:** Graceful handling of edge cases, failovers, and cascading errors.
- **Reliable & Consistent:** High availability guarantees alongside data accuracy and correct state management.
- **Enterprise-Grade:** Built with strict adherence to security, maintainability, and industry compliance.

### Constraints & Formatting
- **Format:** Output the entire document in clean, valid Markdown.
- **Tone:** Objective, precise, and deeply technical.
- **Approach:** Avoid generic advice. Provide specific architectural choices, technology stacks, and data models tailored exactly to the PRD's constraints.

### TDD Structure to Follow
1. **Executive Summary & Goals:** Brief overview, high-level objectives, and explicit non-goals.
2. **System Architecture:** 
 - High-level component diagram (described using Mermaid.js syntax).
 - Core subsystem responsibilities and boundaries.
 - Communication protocols (e.g., gRPC, REST, WebSockets, Event-Driven/Kafka).
3. **Data Architecture & Storage Strategy:**
 - Schema designs or data models.
 - Choice of databases (SQL vs. NoSQL vs. Cache) with explicit justification based on access patterns.
 - Consistency model (e.g., Eventual consistency vs. Strong consistency).
4. **Scalability & Resilience (The "ilities"):**
 - Strategies for handling high concurrency, caching strategy, and rate limiting.
 - Fault tolerance (failover mechanism, circuit breakers, and disaster recovery).
5. **Security & Compliance:** Authentication/Authorization (e.g., OAuth2, RBAC), data encryption (at rest/in transit), and privacy requirements.
6. **Observability:** Metrics, centralized logging, and distributed tracing strategy.
7. **Alternative Solutions Considered:** 2-3 trade-offs explaining why a specific technology or architecture choice was rejected.
